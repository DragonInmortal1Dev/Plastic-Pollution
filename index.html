01 • Understand the problem

Plastic pollution is an ocean problem — and a human problem.

Millions of tonnes of plastic enter aquatic ecosystems every year. Plastic can persist for decades or longer, break into microplastics, harm wildlife and move through food webs. The problem is global, but our everyday choices and collective action can help reduce it.

11M+

tonnes of plastic

More than 11 million metric tonnes of plastic enter the ocean each year, according to the UN Environment Programme.

1.6B+

people depend on oceans

Hundreds of millions of people rely directly on marine and coastal ecosystems for food, jobs and livelihoods.

02 • Why should we care?

Because the consequences reach far beyond the beach.

Where does it come from?

  • Single-use packaging and disposable products
  • Poor waste collection and management
  • Litter that is carried by wind and rivers
  • Overconsumption and a “throwaway” culture

What does it cause?

  • Marine animals can become entangled or ingest plastic.
  • Plastic breaks down into smaller microplastics.
  • Coastal areas and tourism can be negatively affected.
  • Communities face costs from pollution and waste management.

“If not us, who? If not now, when?”

Young people do not need to wait to become leaders. We can lead by acting today.
03 • Youth in action

Young people are not just the future. We are part of the solution now.

Youth can turn awareness into measurable action. A campaign becomes powerful when students work together, influence their school community and make sustainable choices visible and normal.

♻️ Start at school

Organize a waste audit, improve recycling points, reduce single-use plastics and create student-led awareness activities.

📢 Use your voice

Create posters, short videos and social media content. Share facts, challenge myths and encourage friends to participate.

🧤 Take action locally

Join or organize community clean-ups. Record the amount and type of waste collected to show your campaign’s impact.

🛍️ Change habits

Carry a reusable bottle or bag, avoid unnecessary single-use items and choose products with less packaging when possible.

Imagine this:

A group of students notices that hundreds of disposable cups and wrappers are thrown away at school every week. Instead of simply complaining, they measure the waste, create a campaign, propose reusable alternatives and ask classmates to participate. That is youth advocacy: identifying a problem, organizing people and creating a practical solution.

04 • Your turn

You do not have to change the entire world in one day. Start with one action, invite someone else, and build a community that keeps going.
